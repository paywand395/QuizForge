## Primary immunodeficiency is usually present:
- [x] At birth, due to a genetic disorder
- [ ] After chronic malnutrition
- [ ] After steroid therapy
- [ ] Only in old age
> Primary immunodeficiency is present at birth and is usually genetic.

## Compared with primary immunodeficiency, secondary immunodeficiency is:
- [ ] Much rarer
- [x] Much more common
- [ ] Always genetic
- [ ] Only seen in children
> Secondary immunodeficiency is much more common.

## Which of the following is NOT listed as a cause of secondary immunodeficiency?
- [ ] Chronic renal failure
- [ ] Cytotoxic therapy
- [ ] Widespread malignancy
- [x] X-linked agammaglobulinemia
> X-linked agammaglobulinemia is a primary (genetic) immunodeficiency.

## X-linked agammaglobulinemia is also called:
- [ ] DiGeorge syndrome
- [x] Bruton disease
- [ ] Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome
- [ ] Chediak-Higashi syndrome
> Bruton disease = X-linked agammaglobulinemia.

## Bruton disease is caused by failure of:
- [ ] T-cell development in the thymus
- [x] B-cell maturation
- [ ] Both B and T cell development
- [ ] Complement production
> Immature B cells cannot differentiate into mature B cells.

## Bruton disease is seen primarily in:
- [x] Males
- [ ] Females
- [ ] Elderly people
- [ ] Both sexes equally
> It is X-linked, so it is seen primarily in males.

## Patients with Bruton disease mostly suffer from recurrent:
- [ ] Viral infections
- [ ] Fungal infections
- [x] Bacterial infections
- [ ] Parasitic infections
> In most cases there are recurrent bacterial infections.

## The most common of all primary immunodeficiency states is:
- [ ] SCID
- [ ] Bruton disease
- [x] Isolated IgA deficiency
- [ ] AIDS
> Isolated IgA deficiency is the most common primary immunodeficiency.

## In isolated IgA deficiency, the other immunoglobulins are:
- [x] Normal
- [ ] Absent
- [ ] All reduced
- [ ] All increased
> Serum IgA is markedly reduced, but other immunoglobulins are normal.

## Most cases of isolated IgA deficiency are:
- [ ] Fatal in childhood
- [x] Asymptomatic and detected accidentally
- [ ] Presenting with severe meningitis
- [ ] Presenting with lymphoma
> Some patients have recurrent respiratory infections and diarrhea.

## SCID may be inherited as:
- [ ] Autosomal dominant only
- [x] A recessive disorder, autosomal or X-linked
- [ ] Mitochondrial inheritance
- [ ] It is never inherited
> SCID is recessive, either autosomal or X-linked.

## SCID is due to failure of development of:
- [ ] B cells only
- [ ] T cells only
- [x] Both B and T cells from primitive stem cells
- [ ] Neutrophils
> Both B-cell and T-cell development fail.

## Laboratory findings in SCID include:
- [x] Very low lymphocyte count and low immunoglobulins
- [ ] High lymphocyte count and normal immunoglobulins
- [ ] Only low IgA
- [ ] High immunoglobulins
> Children present early in life with recurrent infections.

## AIDS is caused by:
- [ ] A DNA virus
- [x] A retrovirus (HIV)
- [ ] A bacterium
- [ ] A prion
> AIDS is a retroviral disease caused by HIV.

## Which is NOT one of the three main features of AIDS?
- [ ] Opportunistic infections
- [ ] Secondary neoplasms
- [ ] Neurological manifestations
- [x] Autoimmune thyroiditis
> AIDS: severe immunosuppression, secondary neoplasms and neurological disease.

## Which HIV type is more common and most virulent?
- [x] HIV-1
- [ ] HIV-2
- [ ] Both equally
- [ ] HIV-3
> HIV-2 is less common and limited to some parts of Africa.

## Which is NOT a major route of HIV transmission?
- [ ] Sexual contact
- [ ] Dirty needles
- [ ] Mother to newborn
- [x] Shaking hands
> HIV is not transmitted by usual personal contact like hand-shaking or hugging.

## The largest risk group for AIDS (46% of reported cases) is:
- [x] Homosexual or bisexual males
- [ ] Intravenous drug abusers
- [ ] Heterosexual contacts
- [ ] Blood transfusion recipients
> Intravenous drug abusers are 25%; heterosexual contacts 11%.

## Intravenous drug abusers make up about what share of AIDS patients?
- [ ] 1%
- [ ] 11%
- [x] 25%
- [ ] 46%
> IV drug abusers account for about 25% of all patients.

## Among blood recipients, which group is especially at risk?
- [ ] Patients with iron deficiency
- [x] Hemophiliacs receiving factor VIII or IX concentrate
- [ ] Patients receiving albumin only
- [ ] Organ donors
> Especially those who received large amounts of factor VIII or IX.

## In children under 13, AIDS usually results from:
- [x] Mother-to-infant transmission
- [ ] Blood transfusion
- [ ] Insect bites
- [ ] Sexual contact
> The remaining ~10% are hemophiliacs and other blood recipients.

## The main mode of HIV infection worldwide is:
- [ ] Blood transfusion
- [x] Sexual transmission
- [ ] Vertical transmission
- [ ] Needle-stick injury
> Sexual transmission is the main mode worldwide.

## In sexual transmission, the virus enters the body through:
- [ ] Intact skin
- [x] Tears or abrasions in the genital mucosa
- [ ] Saliva only
- [ ] Inhalation
> HIV in semen enters through tears or abrasions in the genital mucosa.

## In the United States, male-to-female heterosexual spread is about how many times more common than female-to-male?
- [ ] 2 times
- [ ] 5 times
- [x] 20 times
- [ ] 100 times
> In Africa and parts of Asia, female-to-male risk is much higher.

## Sexual transmission of HIV is enhanced by coexisting:
- [x] STDs with genital ulceration (herpes, chancroid)
- [ ] Hypertension
- [ ] Iron deficiency anemia
- [ ] Viral hepatitis A
> Genital ulcers make HIV entry easier.

## Which group is the main link spreading HIV to the rest of the adult population through heterosexual activity?
- [ ] Hemophiliacs
- [x] Intravenous drug abusers
- [ ] Children
- [ ] Healthcare workers
> Shared needles and syringes spread HIV among IV drug abusers.

## The major cause of pediatric AIDS is:
- [ ] Blood transfusion
- [ ] Sexual abuse
- [x] Vertical (mother-to-infant) transmission
- [ ] Needle-stick injury
> Vertical transmission is the major cause of pediatric AIDS.

## The risk of sero-conversion after a needle-stick with HIV is about:
- [x] 0.3%
- [ ] 3%
- [ ] 30%
- [ ] 1%
> HBV is 30% and HCV is 3% after needle-stick.

## Antiretroviral therapy after a needle-stick should be given within:
- [ ] 1 week
- [x] 24 to 48 hours
- [ ] 2 weeks
- [ ] 1 month
> It can reduce the risk of infection eightfold.

## Which is NOT a component of the HIV core?
- [ ] Capsid protein p24
- [ ] Two copies of genomic RNA
- [ ] Reverse transcriptase
- [x] gp120
> gp120 and gp41 are in the viral envelope, not the core.

## The three HIV enzymes are:
- [x] Protease, reverse transcriptase and integrase
- [ ] Neuraminidase, protease and polymerase
- [ ] Integrase, lipase and kinase
- [ ] DNA polymerase, ligase and helicase
> These three enzymes are in the viral core.

## Antigenic variability of HIV, a barrier to vaccine development, is due to:
- [ ] p24
- [ ] Reverse transcriptase
- [x] Envelope glycoproteins gp120 and gp41
- [ ] Genomic RNA copies
> gp120 and gp41 are essential for infection and cause antigenic variability.

## The most detectable viral antigen used in blood screening is:
- [x] p24
- [ ] p17
- [ ] gp41
- [ ] gp120
> p24 is the capsid protein used in diagnosis and screening.

## HIV enters T-lymphocytes by attaching to:
- [ ] CD8
- [x] CD4
- [ ] CD19
- [ ] CD20
> CD4 is a high-affinity receptor for HIV.

## The two major targets of HIV infection are:
- [ ] Liver and kidney
- [x] Immune system and CNS
- [ ] Heart and lungs
- [ ] Bone marrow and skin
> The immune system and the central nervous system.

## A provirus is:
- [ ] A free virus in the blood
- [x] A viral genome integrated into the host cell DNA
- [ ] A viral enzyme
- [ ] An antibody against HIV
> HIV cDNA integrates into the host genome to form a provirus.

## The normal CD4/CD8 ratio is about 2. In AIDS it becomes:
- [ ] 4 or more
- [ ] Exactly 2
- [x] 0.5 or less
- [ ] 1.5
> Loss of CD4 cells inverts the ratio.

## In the CNS, HIV mainly infects:
- [ ] Neurons
- [x] Microglia (macrophage equivalents)
- [ ] Oligodendrocytes only
- [ ] Ependymal cells
> HIV is mostly carried into the brain by infected monocytes.

## The acute retroviral syndrome begins how long after infection?
- [ ] 1-2 days
- [x] 3-6 weeks
- [ ] 6 months
- [ ] 2 years
> It lasts about 2-4 weeks and resembles other self-limiting viral infections.

## The acute phase of HIV is characterized by:
- [x] High virus production, viremia and seeding of lymphoid tissue
- [ ] Very low viral levels
- [ ] Kaposi sarcoma
- [ ] AIDS-dementia complex
> Symptoms include sore throat, fever, rash, myalgia and fatigue.

## In the chronic phase of HIV:
- [ ] The immune system has broken down
- [x] The immune system is mostly intact, with continuous viral replication
- [ ] There is no viral replication
- [ ] Patients always have severe symptoms
> Replication occurs mainly in lymphoid tissue; patients are mostly asymptomatic.

## Without treatment, most patients reach the crisis phase within:
- [ ] 6 months
- [ ] 1-2 years
- [x] 7-10 years
- [ ] 20-30 years
> Rapid progressors reach it in only 2-3 years.

## Patients who never reach the crisis phase even without treatment are called:
- [ ] Rapid progressors
- [x] Nonprogressors
- [ ] Carriers
- [ ] Seroconverters
> Rapid progressors reach the crisis phase after 2-3 years.

## Opportunistic infections cause about what proportion of deaths in AIDS?
- [ ] 20%
- [ ] 50%
- [x] 80%
- [ ] 100%
> Opportunistic infections are responsible for about 80% of deaths.

## The most common secondary infection of the CNS in AIDS is:
- [ ] Cryptococcal meningitis
- [x] Toxoplasmosis
- [ ] Herpes simplex
- [ ] Tuberculosis
> Cryptococcal meningitis is also quite frequent.

## The commonest neoplasm in AIDS patients is:
- [x] Kaposi sarcoma
- [ ] Non-Hodgkin lymphoma
- [ ] Cervical carcinoma
- [ ] Hepatocellular carcinoma
> It is commoner in homosexual and heterosexual males than in IV drug abusers.

## In the crisis phase, the CD4+ count falls below:
- [ ] 1500/µl
- [ ] 1000/µl
- [x] 500/µl
- [ ] 2000/µl
> There is a dramatic rise in viremia and clinical disease appears.

## The presenting feature in many AIDS cases is:
- [x] Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia
- [ ] Kaposi sarcoma
- [ ] Cervical carcinoma
- [ ] Bruton disease
> PCP is the presenting opportunistic infection in many cases.

## Disseminated cytomegalovirus infection in AIDS particularly causes:
- [ ] Hepatitis and nephritis
- [x] Enteritis and retinitis
- [ ] Myocarditis
- [ ] Osteomyelitis

## Persistent diarrhea in AIDS is caused by:
- [x] Cryptosporidium or Isospora belli

- [ ] Cryptococcus
- [ ] Candida
> These parasites cause persistent diarrhea.

## Kaposi sarcoma in AIDS patients is associated with:
- [ ] EBV
- [ ] HPV
- [x] Human herpesvirus 8
- [ ] Hepatitis B virus
> KS is a vascular tumor linked to HHV-8.

## The second most common neoplasm in AIDS patients is:
- [ ] Kaposi sarcoma
- [x] Non-Hodgkin lymphoma
- [ ] Cervical carcinoma
- [ ] Leukemia
> They are highly aggressive; 30-40% are associated with EBV.

## Cervical carcinoma in women with AIDS is due to:
- [x] Human papillomavirus
- [ ] Herpes simplex
- [ ] EBV
- [ ] HHV-8
> So gynecologic examination should be routine for HIV-infected women.

## About what proportion of AIDS patients have clinically evident neurologic dysfunction?
- [ ] 5-10%
- [x] 40-60%
- [ ] 80-90%
- [ ] 100%
> Patients may develop a progressive encephalopathy called AIDS-dementia complex.

## HIV vaccine development is difficult mainly because of:
- [ ] Lack of funding
- [x] Huge polymorphism of the virus between patients
- [ ] The virus cannot be grown
- [ ] HIV has no surface proteins
> Prevention and public health measures remain the most effective defense.
